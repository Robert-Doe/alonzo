Grammars & BNF

A grammar is a small set of rules that says exactly which strings are programs. The whole λ-calculus fits in one line.

P4.1BNF in one minute

Backus–Naur Form (BNF), first used for ALGOL 60, writes a language as rules of the form name ::= alternative | alternative | …. Here is the entire syntax of the λ-calculus:

M, N ::= x          variable
       | λx. M      abstraction
       | M N        application

Read it as: "a term (call it M or N) is either a variable, or λ, a variable, a dot and a term, or two terms side by side." The rule refers to itself. That's what lets terms nest to any depth.

Notice this grammar has one alternative per AST node kind (P3.2). That's not a coincidence. A grammar like this, with no precedence information, is called an abstract grammar. It describes trees, not strings.

P4.2The abstract grammar is ambiguous as text

As a description of strings, the rule above is ambiguous. x y z could be (x y) z or x (y z). λx. x y could be (λx. x) y or λx. (x y). Different trees mean different programs, so a real parser needs tie-breaking conventions.

P4.3The two conventions that resolve it

Every λ-calculus text (Church, Barendregt, Pierce's TAPL) uses the same two conventions:

  1. Application is left-associative. x y z means (x y) z. This follows from currying (P1.4).
  2. The body of a λ extends as far right as possible. λx. x y means λx. (x y). You close a λ early only by wrapping it in parentheses: (λx. x) y.

A concrete grammar bakes those conventions in by splitting terms into layers:

term    ::= "λ" ident+ "." term           ← λ grabs everything to its right
          | application
application ::= atom atom*               ← a row of atoms, grouped from the left
atom    ::= ident | "(" term ")"

Module 3's parser is a direct transcription of this grammar: one function per rule.

P4.4Characters → tokens → tree

Parsing usually happens in two passes. A lexer groups characters into tokens (identifiers, λ, ., (, )) and throws away whitespace. Then a parser arranges tokens into a tree using the grammar. Module 2 is the lexer, and Module 3 is the parser.

"λx. x y"  ──lexer──▶  [LAMBDA, IDENT x, DOT, IDENT x, IDENT y, EOF]  ──parser──▶  λx.(x y)
