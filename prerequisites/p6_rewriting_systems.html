PREREQUISITE P6

Rewriting Systems

In the λ-calculus there is no CPU and no memory. Computation is rewriting: find a pattern, replace it, repeat.

P6.1Computation as find-and-replace

You already know a rewriting system: school arithmetic. To evaluate (2 + 3) * 4, you find a piece you know how to simplify (2 + 3), replace it with 5, and repeat: 5 * 4 → 20. Each rule has a left side (the pattern) and a right side (the replacement). The piece that matches a rule's left side is called a redex (reducible expression).

A rewriting system is a word puzzle with a rule card.

The card says things like "anywhere you see AB, you may write C instead." You look for any AB in the current word, replace it, and continue. The card is the whole machine. There is no hidden state.

P6.2Normal forms: where rewriting stops

A term with no redex left is in normal form. You can't rewrite it any further, so it's the "answer." In arithmetic, 20 is a normal form. In the λ-calculus, λx. x is one.

P6.3Choice: which redex first?

(2 + 3) * (4 + 1) has two redexes. You can do either first. Rewriting is non-deterministic: at each step you may have several options. A rule for picking one is called a strategy. Two questions immediately arise:

  • Termination: does every sequence of choices eventually stop? (Not always. In the λ-calculus, some choices loop forever while others finish. That's Module 9.)
  • Confluence: if two different choices both reach a normal form, is it the same normal form? A system with this property is called confluent (or Church–Rosser). Module 10 checks it empirically.
(2+3)*(4+1) ↙ ↘ 5*(4+1) (2+3)*5 ↘ ↙ 5*5 ↓ 25
The "diamond": two different first steps, rejoining later. Confluence says this always happens.

P6.4Arrow notation

NotationMeaning
M → NM rewrites to N in exactly one step
M →* N (also M ↠ N)M rewrites to N in zero or more steps
M = NM and N are connected by steps in either direction (conversion / equivalence)

Subscripts say which rule: →β uses the β-rule, and →η the η-rule.
