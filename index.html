A CODE-FIRST COURSE

Lambda Calculus from Scratch

Build a working λ-calculus engine one small, verifiable module at a time. You start from three kinds of tree node and end with type inference, dependent types, a proof assistant, and a compiler to C and WebAssembly.

How to take this course

  1. Skim the prerequisites. Every tutorial links back to exactly the sections it needs.
  2. For each module, read tutorial.html, run npm test and npm run demo in its folder, then play with its Lab.
  3. Read DECISIONS.md when you want to know why a line is the way it is.

Track 1: The Engine

Phase 1: Bare Terms (Syntax & Binding)

Module 1
Term AST & Printer
Three kinds of node, and the parenthesis rules that make text unambiguous.
Module 2
Lexer
Characters to tokens: maximal munch, positions, located errors.
Module 3
Parser
Recursive descent, the two conventions, and proof the printer never lies.
Module 4
Free Variables & α-Equivalence
Who binds whom, why bound names don't matter, and variable capture.
Module 5
Capture-Avoiding Substitution
M[x := N] done right, plus a vocabulary web for binder, scope, free, bound and capture.
Module 6
de Bruijn Indices
Delete the names: α-equivalence becomes equality, and capture becomes a missed shift.

Phase 2: Computation (Redexes & Reduction)

Module 7
Redex Anatomy
Every β- and η-redex by path; leftmost-outermost, innermost and head.
Module 8
β-Contraction & One-Step Reduction
Decompose, contract, plug: contexts as data, plus a redex/contractum/reduct vocabulary web.
Module 9
Reduction Strategies & Traces
Normal, applicative, CBN, CBV, head: fuel, traces, and who finishes (λx. y) Ω.
Module 10
Reduction Graphs, Residuals & Church–Rosser
Every path at once: forks rejoin, residuals are tracked, and M* closes the triangle.
Module 11
η-Conversion & Normal-Form Zoo
Five kinds of finished (βη, β, head, weak head, value), plus a vocabulary web.

Phase 3: Programming in Pure λ (Encodings)

Module 12
Definitions, Prelude & REPL
Names as macros (they add no power), a prelude, and a REPL in the terminal and the browser.
Module 13
Church Booleans & Pairs
Data as behaviour: booleans are choices, pairs wait for a selector, and if must be lazy.
