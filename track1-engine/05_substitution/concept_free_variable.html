CONCEPT

Free Variable

A name used somewhere no binder of that name encloses it. Its meaning comes from outside the term.

In one sentence

An occurrence of y is free in M if no λy. in M has it in scope. FV(M) is the set of names with at least one free occurrence.

A free variable is a reference to the neighbor down the street.

Nobody in the house coined that nickname. Whoever says it must mean someone outside, and the house can't tell you who. It depends on the neighborhood (the context) the term is placed in.

See it

FV(λx. x y)      = {y}
FV((λx. x) x)    = {x}      ← one x bound, one free: free is per-occurrence
FV(λx y. x)      = {}       ← closed (a combinator)

These are real outputs of freeVars (Module 4 demo). Freeness is relative: y is free in x y, but the same occurrence is bound in λy. x y.

Common mix-up

Wrong

"A free variable is an error, like an undefined variable in a program."

Right

Free variables are perfectly legal. Every sub-term of a closed program has some (the body of λx. x is the term x, with x free). They're just not defined yet at that level. Whole programs are usually closed.

How this connects to the other terms

A free variable is exactly an occurrence that isn't a bound variable: it lies in no scope of a matching binder. Free variables of the substituted term N are what's at risk of capture.
