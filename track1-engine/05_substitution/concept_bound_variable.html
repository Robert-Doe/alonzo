CONCEPT

Bound Variable

A use of a name that refers back to an enclosing binder, and specifically to the nearest one.

In one sentence

An occurrence of x is bound when it lies in the scope of some λx.. It is bound by the innermost such binder.

A bound variable is someone in the room saying the room's nickname.

They mean the guest that this room's door tag named, and nobody else. Rename the tag and all the matching mentions consistently, and nothing about who is meant changes (α-conversion).

See it

(λx. λx. x y) x
          ^       bound by the INNER λx (the one at path fn.body)

Real occurrences() output from Module 4: x at fn.body.body.fn bound by the λ at fn.body. Because bound names only express "which binder," they can be replaced by numbers: "the binder 0 levels out." That's Module 6's de Bruijn indices.

Common mix-up

Wrong

"λx. x y has only bound variables, so it's a closed term."

Right

Bound is a property of each occurrence, and closed is a property of a whole term. y is free here, so the term is open. It's closed only if every occurrence is bound.

How this connects to the other terms

A bound variable sits inside the scope of its binder. Every occurrence is either bound or free, never both. Capture is the bug where a free occurrence silently becomes bound.
