Binder · Scope · Free · Bound · Capture

Five words that get used interchangeably and shouldn't be. Here they are side by side, placed on one picture, with the mix-ups named and corrected.

Definitions, side by side

Binder
The λx. that introduces a name. A declaration, not a use.
λx. x y → "λx"
Scope
The region where a binder's name means its parameter: the body, minus inner rebindings.
(λx. x y) z
Bound variable
A use of a name inside a matching scope. It refers to the nearest such binder.
λx. x y
Free variable
A use with no matching binder around it. Its meaning comes from outside.
λx. x y
Capture
A bug: an operation moves a free name under a same-named binder, and it becomes bound.
(λy. x)[x:=y] ↛ λy. y

One picture: where each term lives

Left: ①–④ on (λx. x y) y. Right: ⑤, capture, and the capture-avoiding fix (real subst output).

Read the chain as: a binder ① owns a scope ②. Uses inside the scope are bound ③. Uses outside every matching scope are free ④. Capture ⑤ is an operation turning a ④ into a ③ by mistake.

Commonly confused pairs

PairThe wrong statementThe correction
binder vs bound variable"In λx. x, both x's are bound variables."The first is the binder (a declaration). Only the second is a bound occurrence. counts(λx. x) reports 1 variable.
scope vs "everything to the right""λx's scope runs to the end of the term."It runs to the enclosing ). In (λx. x) x the last x is outside, so it's free.
free vs undefined/error"A free variable is a mistake."Free variables are legal. Every sub-term of a program has them. Only whole programs are usually closed.
bound vs closed"λx. x y is bound, so it's closed."Bound describes occurrences, and closed describes whole terms. y is free, so the term is open.
capture vs shadowing"λx. λx. x is a capture."That's shadowing: legal nesting you wrote on purpose. Capture is a binding change made by a buggy operation.
free vs "free in M""y is a free variable" (full stop).Freeness is relative to a term. y is free in x y but bound in λy. x y.
