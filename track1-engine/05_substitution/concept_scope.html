CONCEPT

Scope

The region of a term where a binder's name means that binder's parameter.

In one sentence

The scope of λx. is its body, minus any inner region where another λx. takes over the name (shadowing).

A scope is the floor plan of the room behind the name-tag door.

Inside the room, "x" means our guest. Step out through the closing parenthesis and the tag no longer applies. An inner room with its own x-tag is a hole in our floor plan: in there, "x" means their guest.

See it

(λx. x (λx. x) y) x
     ^^^^^^^^^^^        scope of the OUTER λx: its body x (λx. x) y …
            ^             … except here: this x belongs to the INNER λx (a hole)
                  ^  outside every scope: this x is free

Scope is decided by the text, not by what happens at run time. That's lexical scope (P2.4). In the engine, occurrences() tracks scope as a stack of binders and looks names up innermost-first (Module 4).

Common mix-up

Wrong

"The scope of a λ is everything to its right."

Right

It's everything to its right up to the enclosing ), minus inner regions that rebind the same name. In (λx. x) x the final x is outside the scope.

How this connects to the other terms

Every binder owns exactly one scope. A use of the name inside it is a bound variable. A use outside every matching scope is a free variable. Capture is what happens when substitution drops a free name into a scope that happens to use the same name.
