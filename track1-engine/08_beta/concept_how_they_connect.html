Redex · Contractum · Context · Reduct

Four words for the four pieces of one computation step, placed side by side and on one picture, with the mix-ups named and corrected.

Definitions, side by side

Redex
The sub-term about to be rewritten: a rule's left side, found somewhere inside a term.
Contractum
What a redex turns into: the rule's right side, e.g. M[x := N] for β.
Context
The whole term with a hole where the redex sits. It records where the step happens.
Reduct
The whole term after the step: the context with the contractum plugged into its hole.

One picture: where each term lives

One step: C[redex] → C[contractum], and the result is the reduct.

Commonly confused pairs

PairThe wrong statementThe correction
contractum vs reduct"The contractum is the new term."The contractum replaces only the redex (z). The reduct is the whole new term ((λx. x x) z).
redex vs term"This term is a redex, so it reduces."A term can contain redexes without being one: f ((λx. x) y).
context vs substitution"Plugging a hole avoids capture, like substitution."Plugging must capture: λx. □ filled with x is λx. x.
reduct vs normal form"The reduct is the answer."A reduct is one step on. The normal form is the reduct (if any) with no redexes left.
