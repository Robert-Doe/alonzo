CONCEPT

Contractum

What a redex turns into: the rule's right side, e.g. M[x := N] for β.

In one sentence

The contractum is the result of applying the rule to the redex alone: (λx. M) N ↦ M[x := N]. It replaces the redex and nothing else.

The contractum is the envelope's contents with the letter already dropped in.

It's local. It knows nothing about the rest of the pile.

See it

λz. [(λx. λz. x z) z]
  context    C[□] = λz. □
  redex           = (λx. λz. x z) z
  contractum      = λz1. z z1   (renamed z→z1 to avoid capture)
  reduct  C[…]    = λz. λz1. z z1

Real demo output. Module 5's capture avoidance happens right here, while building the contractum.

Common mix-up

Wrong

"The contractum is the new term after the step."

Right

That's the reduct. The contractum only replaces the redex. For (λx. x x) ((λy. y) z) stepping inside, the contractum is z but the new term is (λx. x x) z.

How this connects to the other terms

Contracting a redex gives the contractum. Plugging it into the same context gives the reduct.
