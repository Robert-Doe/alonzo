CONCEPT

Reduct

The whole term after the step: the context with the contractum plugged into its hole.

In one sentence

If M → N, then N is a (one-step) reduct of M: M = C[redex] and N = C[contractum]. With →*, "a reduct" means anything reachable in zero or more steps.

The reduct is the whole pile after you've opened one envelope.

Only one spot changed. Everything else is exactly as it was.

See it

(λx. x x) ((λy. y) z)  →β  (λy. y) z ((λy. y) z)      (redex at ε)
(λx. x x) ((λy. y) z)  →β  (λx. x x) z      (redex at arg)

Real demo output. A term has one one-step reduct per redex.

Common mix-up

Wrong

"A term's reduct is its normal form."

Right

A reduct is just somewhere further along, and a term can have many. The normal form, if there is one, is the special reduct with no redexes left.

How this connects to the other terms

The reduct is the context with the contractum where the redex used to be.
