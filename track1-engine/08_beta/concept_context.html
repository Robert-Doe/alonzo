CONCEPT

Context

The whole term with a hole where the redex sits. It records where the step happens.

In one sentence

A context C[□] is a term with exactly one hole. C[R] fills the hole with R. Every one-step reduction has the form C[R] → C[R'].

The context is the pile of papers with one envelope-shaped gap.

Lift the envelope out, open it, and put the result back in the same gap. The rest of the pile never changes.

See it

λx. [(λy. y) x] z
  context    C[□] = λx. □ z
  rules used      : ξ ∘ app-L ∘ β

In the engine, a context is data with one constructor per congruence rule (appL, appR, lam for ξ), so its shape is the chain of inference rules the step uses.

Common mix-up

Wrong

"Filling a context's hole is substitution, so it must avoid capture."

Right

Plugging is deliberately not capture-avoiding. Filling λx. □ with x gives λx. x, because a redex under a λ must see that λ's variable. Substitution replaces free variables; plugging fills a hole.

How this connects to the other terms

The context surrounds the redex. Swapping the redex for its contractum inside the same context produces the reduct.
